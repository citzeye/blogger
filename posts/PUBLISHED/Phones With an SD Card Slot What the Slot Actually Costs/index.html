Samsung and Motorola are both selling phones with a microSD card slot right now. On one of them the slot is a bonus. On the other, it is the second SIM tray — and putting a card in it costs you a phone line.

Both are advertised as "expandable storage". Nothing on the box, the spec sheet or the product page tells you which one you are buying, because the difference is a single line of jargon in a spec table that is easy to skim past.

What an SD slot actually gives you

It sounds like one feature. It is three, they are sold together, and two of them quietly cost you something else.

  1. Extra storage for media. Photos, video, music. This part works exactly as advertised, up to 1 TB or 2 TB depending on the phone.
  2. Space for your apps. This no longer exists on most phones. More on this below — it is the one people assume they are buying and almost never get.
  3. A second SIM. On many phones the microSD slot and the second SIM share one physical tray. You get one or the other.

The trap: it can cost you your second SIM line

This is the part that matters, and it is stated plainly in Samsung's own specifications. Every regional Samsung store lists the same field for the Galaxy A27 5G:

FieldSamsung Galaxy A27 5G
External Storage Support"MicroSD (Up to 2TB)"
Number of SIM"Dual-SIM"
SIM Slot Type"SIM 1 + Hybrid (SIM or MicroSD)"

Read those three rows together and they contradict each other. The phone is "dual-SIM", and the slot that holds the card is also the slot that holds the second SIM. You have one slot for two purposes. GSMArena's phrasing is the clearest statement of it: "microSDXC (uses shared SIM slot)".

The Galaxy A17 5G is listed identically — "SIM 1 + Hybrid (SIM or MicroSD)" — which means this is a design Samsung uses across its budget range, not a quirk of one model.

Motorola does it differently, and says so

Motorola's spec pages are more honest about the trade. The moto g (2026) and moto g power (2026) both list their SIM configuration in full:

"Dual SIM (1 physical Nano SIM + eSIM + 1 microSD)"

That is one physical SIM, one eSIM, and a separate microSD position — all three at once. The moto g play (2026) product page uses the word "dedicated":

"…the option to expand up to 1TB via a dedicated microSD card slot."

GSMArena describes the Moto G (2026) the same way: "microSDXC (dedicated slot)", with the SIM listed as "Nano-SIM + eSIM".

So the comparison is genuinely like-for-like between two brands selling the same feature at the same price, and the outcome is opposite:

If you travel for work, run a business line alongside a personal one, or want a second number while your existing number stays active, that column is the whole decision.

The other trap: you cannot put apps on it

Even on a phone with a brand-new dedicated card slot, the card holds your photos and your video. It does not hold your apps. This surprises people every year, because it used to work.

Motorola states it on every Moto G support page, without hedging:

"Your phone uses the card as portable storage for media files: photos, videos and music. Because it's portable, you can easily swap the card and its content between devices. … You can't store apps on the SD card because it is portable storage."

The reason is a change Android made in 2019, and it is permanent. Motorola's own general help page still documents the old route, and then explains why it is closed off:

"To move apps to your SD card: 1. Make sure it's formatted as internal storage. … If you don't see this option, the developer does not allow the app to be stored on an SD card."

"If you don't see Format as internal, then your phone only supports SD cards formatted as portable storage."

Its own comparison table makes the gap plain:

Portable storageInternal storage
Store mediayesyes

Content encryptednoyes
Read card in other devicesyesno

"Internal storage" is the mode that allowed apps on the card. Android 10 removed it, and every phone released since has been built as portable-only. Users noticed, on a Moto G Play (2024):

"Unfortunately, starting from Android 10, the ability to format an SD card as internal storage has been removed, and the Moto G Play only supports SD cards as portable storage. This means you can use the card for files like music and videos, but not for installing apps."

Two consequences worth internalising. An app cannot be moved to the card at all, so a phone with 64 GB internal and a 1 TB card still has 64 GB of app storage. And because portable storage is unencrypted by definition — anyone with physical access can pull the card and read it — DRM-protected media often will not move either. Motorola notes this on the moto g power (2026): "may not be possible to move content with DRM restrictions."

Which phones still have one

Worth knowing before you go looking, because the answer is narrower than it was five years ago.

  • Samsung — kept on the Galaxy A-series, dropping from A27 downwards and being removed entirely from the Galaxy S series with the S21 in 2021. The S20 and older have one.
  • Motorola — the Moto G line is the most consistent holdout, and the one that keeps a dedicated slot.
  • Sony — the notable exception among premium makers; the Xperia line still offers expandable storage.
  • Google — no Pixel has ever shipped a microSD slot.
  • Apple — no iPhone has ever had one.

How to check before you buy

It takes one lookup, and the answer is in a field most people never open.

  1. Find the SIM Slot Type field. On Samsung's product pages it sits directly under the storage spec. "Hybrid (SIM or MicroSD)" or "Dual SIM (SIM or MicroSD)" means the card costs you the second SIM. "Dedicated", or a separate SIM + microSD line, means it does not.
  2. Check whether eSIM is supported. On the Samsung models above, the card slot frees up the second SIM but an eSIM can take its place — so dual-SIM survives, just not with two physical cards.
  3. Assume media only. If you are buying a card to make room for apps, that plan does not work on any phone sold today.

So is it worth it?

Genuinely yes, in a specific situation: if you shoot a lot of video, carry offline music or podcasts, or want a gigabyte or two of media you can move to another phone without transferring anything. That is what the slot is for now, and Motorola's own support pages describe it exactly that way — portable media you can swap between devices.

Less yes if you are buying it to avoid a larger storage option at purchase. A 1 TB card is not a substitute for internal storage when apps cannot live on it, and on a hybrid phone you are also giving up a SIM line to get it.

One honest note about our own recommendations: in our budget phone guide we picked the Galaxy A17 5G, and Samsung lists it as "SIM 1 + Hybrid (SIM or MicroSD)". If you run two physical SIM lines today, that pick costs you one of them. If you are on one line, or happy to move the second to an eSIM, nothing changes — but you should know it before checkout rather than after. The rest of that advice stands: it is still a strong phone for the money. Our midrange gaming picks are unaffected, since none of them rely on expandable storage.
