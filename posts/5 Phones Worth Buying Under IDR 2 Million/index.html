Two million rupiah is roughly US$120. That is the budget where most people stop being able to justify a phone purchase, and it is also the budget where the compromises finally start to hurt.

The good news is that this segment has improved a lot. 120 Hz screens, batteries over 5,000 mAh, and multi-megapixel cameras are now standard rather than premium. The bad news is that not every phone at this price is worth buying, and the differences between them matter more here than they do in a higher tier.

What IDR 2 Million Actually Buys You

Before the list, it helps to be honest about the ceiling. At this price you will normally get:

  • A 90 or 120 Hz screen, but almost always IPS LCD rather than AMOLED — except on some Xiaomi and Samsung models, which is a genuine advantage.
  • 6 GB or 8 GB of RAM, paired with 128 GB of storage.
  • A 5,000 mAh battery or larger. Fast charging above 30 W is uncommon here.
  • A capable-enough main camera, usually 50 MP, but with no optical image stabilisation and no real telephoto.

What you will not get at this budget: wireless charging, IP68 water resistance, a genuinely flagship camera system, or more than about two years of Android updates. If any of those four things is a hard requirement, you need a larger budget — no phone below IDR 2 million delivers them.

How to Choose: 4 Things That Actually Matter

Spec sheets at this price are noisy. These four criteria separate the good phones from the bad ones far more reliably.

1. RAM and storage, in that order

Below IDR 2 million, 8 GB of RAM matters more than 256 GB of storage. Extra storage is cheap to add later; you cannot add RAM. A phone with 8/128 GB will feel noticeably faster for longer than one with 6/256 GB, because background apps get reloaded from scratch instead of being kept in memory.

Some brands also offer "extended" or "virtual" RAM, which uses storage as headroom. Treat that as a bonus, not a substitute for real RAM.

2. Battery capacity, and charging speed as a tiebreaker

This is the one area where budget phones genuinely compete. Multiple manufacturers now fit 5,500 to 7,000 mAh cells in this range. Above 5,000 mAh you should expect a full day of heavy use without hunting for a charger.

Charging speed matters more than the raw wattage number suggests: 33 W on a large battery is more usable in practice than 45 W on a small one, because the phone is more likely to be charged only in short bursts.

3. Screen refresh rate, and LCD versus AMOLED

90 Hz versus 120 Hz is the single most visible difference in daily use, and it is not close. Scrolling feels noticeably smoother at 120 Hz.

Between an IPS LCD and an AMOLED at the same refresh rate, AMOLED wins on contrast and colour — but the LCD panels in this class have improved enough that the gap matters far less than it did a few years ago. If the phone is for outdoor use, check peak brightness; anywhere above 1,000 nits is comfortable in direct sun.

4. Software update commitment

This is where the brands split sharply. Samsung and Xiaomi publish explicit update windows; some budget brands do not commit to any timeline at all.

Buy a phone that will still receive security patches in three years, and your phone stays safe and current. Buy one with no stated policy, and you may be running unpatched Android within a year. Over a three-year ownership period, this is arguably the most important of the four criteria.

The 5 Picks

1. Redmi Note 14 — Best All-Rounder

The strongest overall option in this range, and the one we would recommend to most people.

  • Display: 6.67-inch AMOLED, 120 Hz, up to 2,100 nits peak brightness
  • Chipset: MediaTek Helio G99-Ultra
  • Camera: 108 MP main
  • Battery: 5,500 mAh, 33 W charging
  • Price: around Rp 1,895,000 (checked September 2026)

It is the only phone on this list that pairs a 120 Hz AMOLED screen with a large battery at this price, and that combination is what makes it work as a daily driver. High peak brightness means it stays readable outdoors, which matters more than raw megapixel count.

Buy it if you want one phone that handles everything and nothing feels cheap. Skip it if you need heavy gaming — the Helio G99-Ultra is fine for daily tasks but is not a gaming chip.

2. Xiaomi Redmi 15 — Best Battery Life

If you are out of the house all day, charge at an office, or simply forget chargers, this is the one.

  • Battery: 7,000 mAh
  • Display: large display with high refresh rate
  • Price: around Rp 1,749,000 (checked September 2026)

A 7,000 mAh cell in a phone at this price is unusual, and the practical result is that battery anxiety largely stops being a factor. Charging speed is the trade-off: expect it to be on the slower side of this group.

Buy it if battery endurance outranks every other consideration. Skip it if you want fast top-ups and charge overnight.

3. Samsung Galaxy A07 — Best Software Support

Pick this one for longevity rather than specifications.

  • Software: One UI, with Samsung's published update commitment for its A-series
  • Build: slim, understated design
  • Price: around Rp 1,899,000 (checked September 2026)

At roughly the same price as its competitors, the Galaxy A07 is the phone most likely to still be receiving security updates three years from now. If you keep a phone for a long time, that alone justifies choosing it over a better-on-paper rival.

Buy it if you keep phones for years and want a clean, long-supported software experience. Skip it if you want the best raw spec sheet for the money — rivals here have faster screens.

4. Tecno Spark Go 3 — Most Drop-Resistant

The pragmatic choice for anyone who works on-site, travels, or has a history of cracked screens.

  • Durability: IP64 rating, drop resistance tested to about 1.2 metres
  • Display: 120 Hz smooth display
  • Memory: extended RAM support
  • Price: around Rp 1,599,000 (checked September 2026)

An IP64 rating and tested drop resistance at this price is genuinely unusual. A 120 Hz panel means you are not giving up smoothness for durability, which is the usual trade.

Buy it if durability matters more than camera quality. Skip it if photography is a priority — the camera stack here is the weakest of the five.

5. Infinix Hot 60i — Best Value for the Screen

The cheapest route to a 120 Hz display if that is what you are shopping for.

  • Display: 6.7-inch, 120 Hz
  • Price: around Rp 1,499,000 (checked September 2026)
  • Design: thin body

At this price, matching the smooth scrolling of phones costing noticeably more is worthwhile in its own right. You are paying for one specific thing, and you are paying less for it.

Buy it if your usage is messaging, browsing, and video, and you want the smoothest possible screen on the smallest budget. Skip it if you want a longer software runway — check the update policy before committing.

Who Should Skip This Budget Entirely?

Save your money and stretch it if you need any of these:

  • Heavy mobile gaming — you need a dedicated gaming chip, and nothing under IDR 2 million qualifies.
  • Serious photography — a stabilised main camera and a real telephoto lens do not exist at this price.
  • Wireless charging or IP68 water resistance.
  • A phone guaranteed to receive five years of updates.

For most of these, IDR 3 to 4 million is a better starting point than IDR 2 million, because you cross a threshold where the compromises stop being annoying.

How to Check the Price Before You Buy

Prices in this segment move constantly, and promotions shift them week to week. A few habits make the difference between a good deal and an inflated one:

  1. Check the official store first, then compare marketplace listings. Marketplace sellers undercut the official price, but warranty handling differs.
  2. Compare variants, not just models. A 6/128 GB and an 8/256 GB of the same phone can differ by enough to change which one is the better buy.
  3. Look for the storage tier that matches your RAM, rather than the largest storage available.
  4. Be suspicious of prices far below the rest of the market — grey-market units may not carry local warranty.

Which One Should You Pick?

Final Verdict

If we had to pick one phone from this entire budget, it would be the Redmi Note 14. The combination of a 120 Hz AMOLED screen, a 5,500 mAh battery, and a chipset that handles daily tasks without complaint is the most complete package available at this price.

But the right choice depends on what you have already decided matters most to you. Battery endurance points to the Redmi 15. Longevity points to the Galaxy A07. Durability points to the Tecno Spark Go 3. And if the display is the reason you are shopping, the Infinix Hot 60i gets you there for less.

Whichever you pick, re-check the price on the day you buy. The figures in this article are what these phones cost when it was written in September 2026, and in this segment the same phone can be a genuine bargain one week and overpriced the next.
