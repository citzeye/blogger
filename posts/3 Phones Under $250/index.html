Two hundred and fifty dollars used to buy a phone you would not be embarrassed by. In 2026 it buys something more interesting: a genuinely good phone, provided you know which three, and provided you buy the right one for a reason rather than the one with the biggest number on the box.

It also buys you a warning. One of the most recommended budget phones of the last four years has quietly doubled in price and is no longer a budget phone at all. We will come back to that, because it is the most useful thing in this guide.

These three are sold in both the US and the UK, they cost under $250 at US retailers, and each one wins for a different reason. All prices are approximate US figures in USD, checked September 2026 — confirm on the seller's page before you buy, because prices in this band move weekly.

1. Samsung Galaxy A17 5G — Buy It and Forget It

This is the one to buy if the plan is to keep the phone three or four years and not think about it again. That is not a small advantage — it is the whole advantage.

What you get

  • Software: six years of OS and security updates, which is unmatched at this price by a wide margin
  • Display: 6.7-inch AMOLED, 90 Hz
  • Processor: Exynos 1330
  • Memory: 8 GB, with 128 GB or 256 GB storage depending on the SKU
  • Battery: 5,000 mAh, 25 W charging
  • Camera: 50 MP main
  • Price: roughly $210 to $255 (checked September 2026)

Six years of updates is the headline, and it changes the maths. A cheaper phone you replace in two years costs more over the same period than this one, and it means carrying an unpatched phone in the meantime.

What it costs you

The compromises are real but unremarkable: 90 Hz rather than 120, 25 W charging, and an Exynos chip that is fine for daily use and not for games.

Buy it if you want to buy once and stop thinking about it. Skip it if you want maximum storage or faster charging for the same money — the next pick beats it on both.

2. Xiaomi Redmi Note 14 — Most Hardware for the Money

If you are buying one phone to keep and want the most it can do per dollar rather than the longest support window, this is the value pick.

What you get

  • Storage: up to 256 GB, which is unusual at this price
  • Display: 6.67-inch AMOLED, 120 Hz
  • Processor: MediaTek Dimensity 7025 Ultra
  • Memory: up to 8 GB
  • Battery: 5,110 mAh with 45 W charging — the fastest charging here by a wide margin
  • Price: roughly $195 to $200 (checked September 2026)

For about forty dollars less than the Galaxy, you get a faster screen, faster charging, and more storage. That is a straightforwardly better phone for daily use.

What it costs you

What you give up is the update window. Xiaomi's commitment here is shorter than Samsung's, so this is a phone for someone who upgrades on their own schedule rather than someone who wants to keep it until it dies.

One practical note: this model name covers both 4G and 5G versions with different cameras and chips, and retailers list both. Check the configuration on the listing, not just the title, because the price moves with it.

Buy it if you want the best hardware per dollar. Skip it if you want to keep it as long as possible.

3. Samsung Galaxy A16 5G — Spend as Little as Possible

The floor of the category, and a legitimate choice rather than a compromise you have to justify. It regularly sits well under half the price of a mid-ranger.

What you get

  • Display: 6.7-inch AMOLED, 90 Hz — an AMOLED at this price is the real headline
  • Processor: Exynos 1330
  • Memory: 8 GB, 128 GB storage, expandable
  • Battery: 5,000 mAh, 25 W
  • Camera: 50 MP main
  • Price: roughly $135 (checked September 2026)

What it costs you

It shares the A17's chipset and gets the same class of display, so the difference between them is mostly storage, update commitment and price. For someone replacing a broken phone, or for a second phone for work calls, that is enough.

Storage expandable to 1.5 TB via card is unusual and genuinely useful at this price. It is also slower than internal storage, so keep the apps you care about on the internal drive.

Buy it if you need a working phone cheaply and do not want to think about it. Skip it if the money is there, because the A17 above is a strict upgrade for a modest difference.

The Budget Phone That Stopped Being Budget

What happened to the Moto G line

If you have read budget phone guides recently, the name you will keep seeing is the Motorola Moto G Power. In April 2026 Motorola raised US prices across the whole Moto G line overnight, driven by memory chip costs moving through the industry.

The Moto G Power went from $299.99 to $399.99. That is no longer a budget phone by any reasonable definition — it costs more than the Galaxy A17 and is a weaker phone than the Redmi.

We are not singling Motorola out. The same pressure is visible across the category, and it is the reason every price in this article is a range with a check date rather than a single number. But if a guide — including an older version of this one — recommends a model on the strength of what it used to cost, treat that recommendation as expired.

Where to Buy in the US and UK

All three models are mainstream products sold in both markets, so nothing here requires importing.

  • US: Best Buy, Amazon, Walmart, and the manufacturer's own store carry all three. Check the manufacturer's store first, then compare marketplace listings — sellers undercut the official price, but warranty handling differs.
  • UK: Currys, Argos, Amazon UK, and the manufacturer's UK store carry the Samsung and Xiaomi lines. UK prices are quoted in GBP and generally sit higher than the US equivalents, so check the UK price rather than assuming a converted figure. You will also need a UK three-pin plug.
  • Watch the configuration. These product lines ship in several memory and storage combinations, and the price moves with each one. A 128 GB model at a good price is a better buy than a 256 GB model at a bad one.

Who Should Spend More

Save your money if you are not shopping for any of these:

  • Heavy mobile gaming — you need a dedicated gaming chip, and nothing under $250 qualifies.
  • Serious photography — a stabilised main camera and a real telephoto lens do not exist at this price.
  • Wireless charging, or IP68 water resistance.
  • A phone guaranteed five years of updates — only the Galaxy A17 here is.

If you need any of those, the budget moves to roughly $400 to $700, and you cross a threshold where the compromises stop being annoying.

Final Verdict

If we had to pick one phone from this entire budget, it would be the Samsung Galaxy A17 5G. Six years of updates at roughly the price of a mid-ranger is the single most useful thing in this category, and it is the spec you cannot add later.

But the right choice depends on what you have already decided matters most. If you want the most hardware per dollar, the Redmi Note 14 gives you a faster screen and more storage for less money. If you need a working phone cheaply and do not want to think about it, the Samsung Galaxy A16 5G is a real answer rather than a compromise.

Whichever you pick, re-check the price on the day you buy, and confirm the exact configuration. In this category the same phone can be a genuine bargain one week and overpriced the next.
