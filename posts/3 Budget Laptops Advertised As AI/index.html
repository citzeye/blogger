Three laptops, about three hundred dollars each, and every single one of them is sold with the word "AI" on the box. Not one of them contains the chip that makes a computer do AI.

That is not a subtle technicality. It is the whole question, and almost every buyer finds out about it from a forum post three weeks after they paid. So here is the short version before the details: these machines can run AI in a browser, and they can run a small AI model slowly on the processor, and that is the complete list.

We took the same three budget laptops from our school laptop guide and checked what each one can genuinely do without sending your work to a data center. The answer is more nuanced than "no", and more useful than the sticker on the box suggests. If you are considering one of them because you want local AI, read the section on what to spend instead — it saves you a genuinely expensive mistake.

The Short Answer: None of Them Have an NPU

A neural processing unit is a separate chip built for one job: crunching the matrix multiplications that machine learning is made of. A CPU can do that work too, just far more slowly and at a cost in battery. When a manufacturer says a laptop has AI acceleration, this is the part they mean.

All three of these laptops run processors with no NPU at all — not a disappointing one, none:

  • The Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 3i has an Intel Core i3-N305 or an Intel N100. Both are Alder Lake-N chips with no NPU.
  • The HP OmniBook 3 has an entry AMD Ryzen 3. No NPU.
  • The Acer Chromebook Plus 514 ships with an Intel Core 3 N-series or Ryzen 3 depending on configuration. Neither has an NPU.

That single fact decides most of the rest of this article.

What "Running AI" Actually Requires

Microsoft's Copilot+ PC label is the clearest public benchmark for what counts as a laptop that does AI on-device. It requires three things simultaneously:

  • An NPU rated at 40 trillion operations per second or more
  • At least 16 GB of memory
  • At least 256 GB of storage

These three laptops fail the first requirement and, at 8 GB, the second. They are not near-misses on this. They are a different category of computer.

The practical consequence is concrete: the Windows features that only run on an NPU — Live Captions with real-time translation, the Recall timeline, background image generation in Paint, automatic upscaling in Photos — are not available on any of these machines. Not slow. Absent.

What They Can Actually Do

Zero NPU does not mean zero AI. It means CPU-only AI, which is slow but real. Three things work.

Cloud assistants, fully

Copilot, Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT run in a browser tab and are perfectly usable on all three laptops, because the work happens on someone else's server. This is what almost every "AI laptop" review from the last few years has actually been describing.

The trade is the one you would expect: you are paying for compute you do not own, and the tool only works while you have a connection.

Small language models on the CPU

This is the genuinely interesting option. Software like llama.cpp, and the tools built on it such as Ollama and LM Studio, can run a quantized language model on nothing but a processor. It requires no GPU, no API key, and no internet connection once the model is downloaded.

How slow is slow? One measured data point for calibration: a 2018 ultrabook processor with four cores managed about 5.6 tokens per second running a 7 billion-parameter model at the lowest useful quantization. The N305 in the Lenovo is a much newer chip with eight cores, so it lands in the same general range rather than a different one.

That is fast enough to be useful and far too slow to feel like ChatGPT. Realistically this is for summarizing a document, drafting from a template, or working with text where nothing leaves your machine. It is not a replacement for a good cloud assistant.

Two hardware details decide whether this goes well. Both N-series chips support AVX2, which is what the fast quantized kernels need, and both leave AVX-512 off, so the very fastest paths are closed. More importantly, both have single-channel memory — a real bottleneck when a model larger than a few gigabytes has to stream weights from RAM.

Everyday machine learning that already runs

Some AI is light enough that these chips have always done it. Live Captions and transcription, on-device text-to-speech, translation, and the on-device dictation in Zoom all work, because they are small models that fit in a few hundred megabytes.

This is the part laptop marketing usually implies and rarely explains. A Chromebook has run basic on-device speech and language features for years with no NPU whatsoever. Nothing about that changed.

When You Should Spend More

If on-device AI is a requirement rather than a curiosity, the money has to move, and here is roughly where it moves to:

  • Around $700 and up is where the first genuinely capable NPU laptops appear. The Acer Aspire 16 AI and the HP OmniBook 5 Next Gen AI PC both sit in this range and carry qualifying neural hardware.
  • Above $900 opens up the faster Snapdragon-based machines, whose NPUs are among the most capable currently shipping.

The jump from three hundred to seven hundred dollars is real money, and for most people buying a laptop for school it is money that buys a better screen, more memory and a longer warranty instead. That is a defensible trade.

The case for spending it is narrower and worth stating plainly: if your coursework involves running models, if a data privacy rule stops you sending material to a cloud service, or if you intend to experiment with local AI as a hobby, then an NPU laptop is not a luxury. It is the minimum viable tool.

What to Skip

Save yourself a wasted purchase by ignoring these:

  • Any listing that calls one of these three "AI powered" without naming a processor with an NPU. There is no such chip inside.
  • Listing "AI PC" or "AI laptop" in a product title. Microsoft reserves Copilot+ for hardware that meets a specific NPU threshold, and these do not meet it.
  • Expectations that a small CPU can run a large model quickly. It cannot, on any of these, and no amount of configuration changes that.

Final Verdict

If you want to run AI on your own machine without paying for cloud compute, the honest answer across all three is the same: you can run small models slowly, or use cloud assistants quickly, and you cannot do the thing that "AI laptop" usually implies.

What that costs you is the list above — about seven hundred dollars for the first NPU-equipped machines. Whether it is worth it depends entirely on why you wanted it. For writing, browsing and coursework, the budget laptops from our school laptop guide are simply better value, and cloud assistants already cover the AI-shaped parts of the job.

Whichever you pick, re-check the price on the day you buy, and confirm the exact configuration.
