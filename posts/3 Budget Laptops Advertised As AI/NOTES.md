THE HONEST ANSWER, which is the whole point of this article:
  None of these three has a neural processing unit. Not a weak one, none. The
  question "can it run AI" therefore has a mostly-negative answer, and saying so
  plainly is what makes the piece worth reading. Do NOT write this as a "yes,
  here are 3 AI laptops" roundup — that would be false and would put a wrong
  product claim next to a real brand name.

SOURCED FACTS used in the article (do not restate these from memory):
  - Microsoft requires a 40+ TOPS NPU plus 16 GB RAM and 256 GB storage for the
    Copilot+ PC label. Source: microsoft.com/en-us/windows/copilot-plus-pcs,
    support.microsoft.com Copilot+ PC hardware requirements.
  - Intel Core i3-N305: 8 cores / 8 threads, up to 3.8 GHz, 15 W TDP, UHD
    Graphics 770, single-channel memory, 32 GB max. NO NPU. Alder Lake-N,
    released Q4 2022. Source: techpowerup.com CPU database.
  - Intel Processor N100: 4 cores / 4 threads, up to 3.4 GHz, 6 W TDP, 16 GB
    max memory. NO NPU. Source: techpowerup.com CPU database.
  - AVX2 is present; AVX-512 is NOT, on both N-series chips. This matters for
    local inference because the quantized kernels that matter most want AVX2.
  - llama.cpp inference speed is governed by tokens per second, and the tooling
    around it (Ollama, LM Studio) is built on it. Source: github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp
  - A 2018 ultrabook CPU (i7-8550U, 4 cores) managed 5.64 tok/s on a 7B model at
    Q4_0 quantization. Source: mgroeber.de llama.cpp benchmarking, Feb 2026.
    This is the ONLY measured figure quoted, and it is deliberately framed as an
    older machine, because a current budget chip is in the same ballpark for
    this workload. Do NOT invent a tokens-per-second number for the N305.

WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED ANYWHERE IN THIS ARTICLE:
  - No Copilot+ label for any of the three.
  - No measured AI benchmark for any of the three.
  - No token rates for any of the three.
  - No "runs AI" without a qualifier.
